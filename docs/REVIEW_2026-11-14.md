## The reframe: TIME and COLOUR
The pitch treats SPHEREx's changing wavelength as a defect ("the star flickers for no real reason"). The colour is the wonder. Build the product around two controls:
- **TIME:** flip the visits at one fixed colour. The star moves along a trail, and a toggle shows "only what changed" (`diff.py` exists).
- **COLOUR:** sweep 0.75 to 5 um. The image tints by wavelength, and the star's spectrum draws itself point by point beside it.

One line for judges: "IRSA shows every frame. Blink shows the two things that change: where it is, and what colour it is."

## Landing page, first 10 seconds

- One sentence: "This star moved. SPHEREx saw it: 10.5 +/- 0.7 arcsec a year northward. Gaia says 10.4."
- Below it: "Try any star or coordinates", plus five target chips.
- Then a short "why this matters" paragraph, before the tool (pattern from SpaceGenes+, 2025 Science winner).

## Second hero: Epsilon Indi B
Its meta.json shows 37.6, 13.6 and 5.7 mJy at 1.30, 1.33 and 1.37 um: a steep drop. That is the water absorption band expected in a brown dwarf. The caption would be: "This dip is water vapour, in a failed star 12 light years away." An astronomer should confirm that wording before it ships.

## What past winners did (opened pages, 2024 to 2025)
1. **Open on real data in motion, not a title card.** The SkyShield orrery (2024 Science winner) drops you straight into a running 3D model.
2. **Check yourself against one published result.** Resonant Exoplanets (2025 Data winner) shows a "Validated Discovery" card citing a paper. Our Gaia comparison and IRSA spectrum check are exactly this. Make them visible.
3. **30-second video structure:** problem, cost, "so we built", one number, close. No team intro. ECI Centauri opened with a team intro and was a finalist, not a winner.
4. **Name exact datasets and counts.** For us: "about 1.6 million SPHEREx images, QR2 and QR3".
5. **A few controls, each mapped to a physical quantity, plus a time scrubber.**
6. **Show the method openly:** a numbered pipeline and a validation page, kept off the main view.
7. **Keep the demo alive** after judging, and include a recorded fallback.
8. **A dark space theme is near universal.** Our Observatory look has to stand out through instrument detail, not darkness.

## Fix before anything is public (confirmed in code)
1. **Live mode skips wavelength matching.** `api/live.py` calls `build_target` without `match_wavelength`, so any-coordinates results are the naive flipbook the pitch attacks.
2. **Live mode ignores how far stars have moved since their catalogue position was taken.** Gaia is searched only 5 arcsec around the typed coordinates, in a 2 arcmin field. The challenge's own WISE 0855 coordinates (epoch about 2010) and Barnard's J2000 position land on empty sky. Fix: search Gaia several arcmin around for high-motion sources propagated back to J2000, and default to a 6 arcmin field.
3. **The data releases are not separated.** `find_frames` has no `obs_collection` filter, so the same exposure may appear twice across QR2 and QR3. Needs checking.
4. **The WISE 0855 wording overstates it.** Forced photometry is about 2 sigma per visit (for example 0.39 +/- 0.20 mJy). Corrected wording: "Not separately detected; about 2 sigma per visit, consistent with the expected 0.47 mJy plus a blended background star."
5. **Epsilon Indi B, Apr 2026 visit.** Its stack is at 1.30 to 1.37 um, while the other visits are at 1.23 to 1.29 um, so the star dims in the loop from colour alone. Drop the visit from TIME or label it.
6. **R Draconis shows as a donut,** because saturated cores are rendered black. Fill them and mark them "too bright".
7. **The caption "Gaia: 10.4" is the northward component only.** Say "northward drift".
